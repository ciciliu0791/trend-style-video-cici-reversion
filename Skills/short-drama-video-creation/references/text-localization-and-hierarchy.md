# Text, localization and visual hierarchy

## Assign information roles

For each text-heavy beat, choose the primary focus: performance, operation or result. State what dialogue adds and what an explanatory card adds. Remove, shorten or stagger redundant layers rather than showing the same explanation in dialogue, UI and a central card simultaneously.

Frame, opacity, color and language order belong to the project style profile. Record its version in the brief; do not prescribe transparent captions, bilingual text or a particular palette for every film. If using translucent panels, keep text independently legible instead of lowering the opacity of the whole group. A still behind animated UI remains a still-backed graphics segment, not new character performance.

## Define language coverage before layout

When localization is requested, inventory dialogue, speaker labels, animated interfaces, notifications, messages, statuses, buttons, explanatory cards and end titles. Record which surfaces need which languages, their order and typography. Preserve proper brands, code and identifiers where translation is inappropriate.

Maintain a project glossary for character display names and recurring terms. Internal asset IDs may retain legacy names, but audience-facing labels must follow the approved glossary. Do not add absent characters merely to display the roster.

Translate concisely without changing dates, amounts, negation, permissions or the joke. Reflow cards and spacing after translation; do not keep shrinking text to fit. Split long dialogue into semantic units when needed, preserving the approved pacing or flagging a necessary timing change. Languages share the same reveal beat; typing speeds may differ, but both must finish with reading time before the next state. Translation must not reveal an outcome early.

Use a shared text source or reconcile the timeline, graphics and subtitle sidecar after changes. Plain SRT does not preserve exact visual placement or styling. Check font fallback and punctuation in the actual render, not only in source text.

## Protect both platform space and dramatic action

Define a safe area for the intended platform, aspect ratio and viewing container. Check the entire bounds of text, name labels, outlines, shadows and panels. A caption origin inside the safe area does not prove the caption fits. Treat platform margins as project presets, not universal guarantees; record real publishing-preview checks separately from simulations.

Check obstruction through the entire shot: faces and expression peaks, contact points, relevant documents and operation details must remain visible. Keep caption placement stable within a shot unless movement is deliberately designed. If no clear space exists, shorten or stagger information, change placement or revise the layout; do not solve bottom-UI obstruction by covering the face.

Review at intended viewing size, including a phone-size preview for mobile delivery. Sample caption entry, longest translations, speaker changes, animation before/during/after a change and completed states. Check reading time as well as clipping, contrast and overlap. Static samples do not replace normal-speed playback.

## Conditional: product and workflow demonstrations

Apply this section when technical facts or simulated operations carry the story, not to unrelated fiction.

Trace `input → operation → visible result`. Reconcile quantities, amounts, duplicate inputs, permissions and unresolved information; additional evidence is not automatically a new transaction. Do not display a result before its cause. Physical props may have credible texture and density without exact readable text; render essential numbers and labels in postproduction.

Identify simulated UI as such where confusion is likely. Verify claims about product capabilities, platforms and permissions against current authoritative evidence, recording source/date and unresolved limits. A note describing the platform used for this demonstration must not imply that all products or plugins share the same restrictions. Keep volatile product facts in the project evidence, not as permanent skill rules.
