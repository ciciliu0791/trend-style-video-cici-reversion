# Creative and editorial review

Diagnose this film rather than forcing a universal aesthetic. Keep notes concrete: what happens, what the viewer can infer, and what is missing.

## Story and performance

For drama, inspect desire, resistance, action, reaction, consequence. Describe behavior, not merely emotions. Instead of “she is devastated,” a possible direction is: “She aligns two cups, hears he will not return, then puts only his cup back in the cupboard.” Use this only if appropriate to the character and scene.

Check information order. Does a reaction precede its cause? Does someone act on information they lack? Does an insert reveal the twist too early? Track audience knowledge separately from character knowledge.

For lyrical/observational work, assess attention, sensory progression, rhythm and intended feeling without inventing conflict. For episodes, separate persistent canon from the episode's temporary situation.

## Coverage and staging

Identify the shot carrying each indispensable beat and why the edit can use it. Check direction, eyeline, hand/prop state, posture, geography, light/time, and emotional progression. Intentional discontinuity can be valid when it supports the intended experience.

Tie framing/movement to viewpoint and changing information. Plan reactions and pauses. Decide whether the scene requires synchronized speech, offscreen voice, silence or physical action before choosing generation structure. Test uncertain critical interactions within authorized scope; a successful face reference does not validate a handoff or exchange.

## Three passes

1. **Experience:** play the complete cut normally; note confusion, attention loss, rushed beats, and weak emotion.
2. **Construction:** diagnose causes at timestamps: absent reaction, premature reveal, mismatched motion, repetition, insufficient handles, missing ambience, buried dialogue.
3. **Delivery:** inspect the actual export, including sound and captions, against destination requirements.

Sampled frames support but do not replace playback. Never claim viewing/listening that tools did not permit. Ask for human review of unverified dimensions while continuing other checks. Agent predictions are not collected viewer feedback.

Use notes in the form: `version/time → observation → audience consequence → smallest repair → recheck`.

Example: `cut-v03 00:18–00:20 → recipient smiles before seeing the envelope → surprise reads as foreknowledge → move reaction after insert using take 02 → replay 00:15–00:24 with sound`.

Classify issues as blocking (unintelligible story/dialogue or missing required beat), major (distracting performance/continuity), or polish. These are project conventions, not certified ratings. Do not average away a blocking defect.

Try editing or an available alternate before more generation when intent is preserved. Do not substitute explanatory subtitles for a missing dramatic event without an accepted creative decision. Recheck the scene in context.

## Evaluate improvement

For a pilot, track comprehension of the intended turn, continuity defects, required-beat completeness, editorial rework, usable generated seconds, spend and elapsed time. Define usable footage by inclusion or genuine editorial suitability, not API success. If comparing workflows, hold brief, budget, model/settings, and review questions as constant as practical and document confounders. Do not promise improvement without production evidence.
