# Director's workbook

Use this when creating or changing direction. It organizes original decisions; it is not a camera preset catalog. Inherit approved material and omit questions already resolved.

## 1. Interpret the material

State what this film is asking the audience to experience and what is specific to these characters and this world. Identify the central tension or sensory progression, perspective, and ending effect. Separate the literal event from its meaning. For drama, identify the scene's change; for observational work, identify what sustained attention reveals.

Deliver a short directing intention that can discriminate between possible shots. “Cinematic and emotional” cannot choose a shot; “we learn that she has forgiven him only when she makes room at the table” can.

## 2. Organize audience knowledge

For each essential beat, decide who the audience is aligned with, what is visible or audible, what remains offscreen, and when new information becomes available. Track character knowledge separately. Design suspense, surprise or ambiguity only when intended.

Compare different approaches only at consequential open decisions. Explain the gain and loss, select one, and preserve it downstream. A stronger alternative must change the viewing experience rather than simply add lens terminology.

## 3. Direct performance and space together

For each important exchange, identify the immediate objective, tactic, trigger and response. Express emotion through actions, listening, hesitation, vocal behavior or stillness. Do not require a fixed set of gestures, tears, or microexpressions.

Block the action: initial positions, access to important objects/exits, approach/retreat, distance, contact, gaze and final relationship. Use props and space where they carry the story. Determine what this requires of the camera, then revise staging and framing together as necessary. Camera changes do not change world geography.

## 4. Establish a visual and sonic language

Choose framing/proximity, camera movement or stillness, light sources, color/material relationships, production design, sound perspective and pace because of their effect on the audience. Record stable rules and meaningful exceptions. Named directors may inform a discussion but are not mandatory style authorities or shortcuts to a coherent film.

For sound, decide what dialogue, ambience, effects, music and silence contribute. Sound can introduce an unseen event, connect spaces, create a contradiction, or carry a transition. Do not add music merely to label emotion.

## 5. Convert direction into coverage

Map required beats to shots. Each shot records its purpose, what viewers discover, playable action and staging, selected view, duration estimate, sound, cut motive, and entry/exit state. Include enough coverage to carry reactions and consequences; avoid redundant coverage for its own sake. A one-take design remains valid.

Separate the film's desired timing from model job boundaries. Maintain the selected design when compiling prompts. If the service cannot execute it, show the practical constraint and propose the smallest creative change rather than silently flattening it.

## 6. Review what reached the screen

Compare the actual assembled scene with the directing intention: is the crucial discovery legible, does behavior carry the turn, do sound and timing support it, and does the next scene inherit the right state? Diagnose whether the failure began in interpretation, staging, coverage, source execution, or editing. Repair that cause instead of adding decorative shots.

Example: a farewell is meant to feel unresolved. A smiling reaction selected too early can imply reconciliation. First inspect reaction timing and alternate takes; do not default to regenerating the room or adding sad music.

## Minimal scene decision

Record: scene purpose; audience entry/exit understanding; character objectives/actions; spatial change; selected image/sound/time approach; covering shots; continuity; unresolved risk. This is the reusable handoff to production, not a mandatory essay for every shot.
