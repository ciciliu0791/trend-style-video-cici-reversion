Shared source-analysis contract for workspace Trend and Viral. Analyze only the supplied evidence, before committing to structure remake or story rewrite. Do not infer the user's mode from the skill name.
When producing scripted/model analysis, return JSON with overview, observations/shots, viral_factors, must_keep, replaceable, risks, characters and limits. For a human-readable review, present the same findings in the existing review entry; do not create a competing analysis or require JSON from the user.
Each observation needs its actual timestamp/range and evidence source. Browser samples do not establish a complete shot list, exact cuts, lyrics or beats. Separate observations from inferred creative mechanisms. Preserve visible text as source analysis; do not decide its production method here.
Source dialogue must come from the supplied verified transcript. If unavailable, leave dialogue unknown; do not infer speech from captions or fabricate it. Do not call a video viral based on visual style alone: popularity evidence may be unknown.
For creative/performance references analyze lead/support staging, formations, camera scale changes, rhythm and causal transitions. Product, price and CTA findings may be not applicable. Do not invent absent claims.

Use the existing fields; do not require new JSON keys or six separate documents:
- overview: the audience experience and image/film treatment, not just the subject or props. Explain the emotional/sensory appeal and how performance, framing and sound position the viewer. Mark inferred audience responses as hypotheses.
- observations/shots: entry state -> subject action -> object response -> exit state, using actual event-centered timestamps. Include meaningful contact, direction, peak, completion and expression when visible; a fixed number of stills is not evidence of continuous choreography.
- viral_factors: the visible/audible carrier of initial interest, sustained attention, contrast/repetition/reveal and ending effect. These are interpretations, not verified causes of popularity.
- must_keep and replaceable: propose which mechanisms depend on action, costume, framing, palette or sound; which surface elements can change without losing them. These are preservation recommendations until the user selects the direction, not authority to lock the source plot in both modes. Explain the function of color, lighting/materials and camera relationships rather than listing adjectives.
- risks and limits: distinguish observed, user-reported, inferred and unknown; record missing playback/listening and which conclusions they affect. If a core hook depends on unheard audio, do not settle that interpretation from images; request relevant evidence or defer it. Continue clearly limited visual reasoning where feasible.
- characters: describe agency and the behaviors that carry the mechanism. Do not turn "actively styling oneself" into "wearing a wig under a dryer" merely because both share props.

Prefer supplied complete playback, event-centered slow viewing/samples and listening as complementary evidence; never claim an inspection the provided inputs do not support. Deliberate stillness, slow pacing or repetition may be the defining mechanism: do not invent escalation, humor, a twist or active movement to make every reference fit one formula.

Cover the following dimensions within those existing fields, without new mandatory keys:

| Dimension | Evidence to identify | Mechanism to explain |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | First attention cue, sustained interest, contrast/repetition/reveal and ending effect | Which action, image or sound carries each; a prop is not a mechanism |
| Audience experience | Performance, intimacy/distance, surprise, tension, calm or sensory pleasure actually supported by evidence | How the film invites the proposed feeling; distinguish observation from inferred effect |
| Discovery and action | Entry state, meaningful change, contact/peak, response and result | What changes the viewer's understanding and what carries attention forward |
| Rhythm | Action duration, holds, cut intervals and verified musical events | Where time compresses or opens; distinguish event density from cut density |
| Camera | Scale, angle, movement, framing and subject relationships | How the view conceals, reveals, explains or redirects attention |
| Design | Palette, light, materials, costume, scale and geography | How visual relationships create contrast, readability and a consistent world |
| Sound | Verified music, voice, ambience, effects and silence | Pulse, perspective, anticipation and sonic payoff; unheard elements stay unknown |

Synthesize a few main attraction points rather than an adjective inventory. For each, include source evidence/time, visible/audible carrier, proposed audience effect, what must survive, replaceable surface details and affected unknowns. Put these in viral_factors and the corresponding must_keep/replaceable entries. A "trending" label is not proof of popularity or retention.

Keep source observations independent of adaptation choices. Both modes retain selected Hook, audience experience, style relationships and attraction mechanisms. Structure remake additionally maps the source progression, shot functions and rhythm; story rewrite may replace the plot, shot order/count and ending. Analysis-only requests need not choose either mode.

Carry the selected findings forward as:
source evidence -> selected mechanism/experience -> preserved or changed action/view/sound -> actual prompt clause -> reference authority -> output check.
Do not silently replace active performance with passive environment motion, erase intentional quietness, or drop required beats to simplify production. If a required reference is missing, name it and its affected beat and seek the user's input before considering omission.
